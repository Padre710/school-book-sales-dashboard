**I turned 606 handwritten invoices into a dashboard, and was asked to check only 9.7% of them.**

A small publisher in Lagos kept 8 years of sales in carbon-copy invoice books. The photos were read by an AI into a spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet was full of small errors: a 6 read as an 8, a name misspelled, one invoice attached to the photo of its neighbour.

The rule I set: never trust one reading, never guess.
- Every photo was read again by independent readers that never saw the spreadsheet.
- A value was accepted only if an independent reading agreed with it.
- 32 written rules fixed what the arithmetic could prove (quantity x price = line total, lines add up to the total, one price that gives a whole number of books).
- Only what no rule could settle went to the owner, one invoice at a time, with the photo.

When the first year was done fully by hand, the owner had to check 71% of the invoices. For the other seven years it was 9.7%: every answer became a new rule.

Then a last review found three errors the rules had missed, including a batch of invoices counted in two years. A fix is not finished until a different check says so.

What the data says:
- September and August bring 56% of revenue: the new school year.
- Invoices fell from 122 to 34 a year, but the average invoice grew from ₦13,231 to ₦46,722.
- One typical title went from ₦220 to ₦620 a copy: prices did much of the growth.
- 2020, the COVID year, fell 56% and then rebounded 137%.
- Schools are 76% of revenue; no single customer carries the business.

Everything is public (anonymised): the data, the 32 rules, the checks and the dashboard.
